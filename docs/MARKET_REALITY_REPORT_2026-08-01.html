The answer first

Is it worth developing?

Yes — but not for the reason you might hope, and not on the current path. The verdict splits three ways:

Yes — already As your own trading instrument & engineering asset

It already passes its own bar ("which trading decision does this improve?"). Nothing on the market combines your live single-view read with a decision ledger. The codebase is also an exceptional portfolio piece — the delivery discipline behind it is rarer than the product.

Conditional As an indie business (~$1–5k MRR class)

Viable — Gexbot, Volland and OptionStrat prove solo founders live here — but only if you now spend most effort on distribution, fix a data-licensing landmine before your first paying user, and reposition around the two things nobody does: freshness and a public track record.

Realistically no As a venture-scale company

The niche's only public P&L (BlackBoxStocks: $2.57M revenue, shrinking 17%/yr at ~$100/mo) shows the ceiling of the mid-band. Customers structurally churn — 70–90% of new retail traders wash out within a year. This is a lifestyle-business market with one meme-powered outlier.

The single most important sentence in this report

Your bottleneck is not the product. Convexa has more shipped, more honest, and better-engineered surface than several tools charging $99/mo — and zero users, zero distribution channel, and a live site five weeks behind the code. Every survivor in this niche won with a free-content flywheel (X + Discord + a free daily artifact) built before monetization. Feature work is now the comfortable form of procrastination.

Evidence layer

The honest truth about what you're selling

1 · The mechanism is real; the cheap metric mostly isn't

Dealer gamma-hedging feedback is validated in peer-reviewed literature — Baltussen et al. (Journal of Financial Economics, 2021) across 60+ futures markets, Barbon & Buraschi ("Gamma Fragility") for equities. But the version an indie app can cheaply compute — overnight OI × the "dealers are long calls, short puts" sign convention — is exactly the version the strongest evidence undermines: OptionMetrics showed net-sign GEX fails to explain vol/returns once you split dealer books; Cboe's own flow data shows 0DTE customer flow is roughly balanced, so OI-sign GEX estimates a quantity that may be near zero with a huge error bar; an independent backtest found raw GEX had no predictive power (vol-normalized GEX did). Vendors themselves concede this in their explainers.

What this means for Convexa

If you ship naive OI-GEX as the headline, you are selling the discredited version of a true idea — same as SpotGamma, UW and MenthorQ, and their users' #1 complaint ("works in hindsight", "lagging") is the symptom. The defensible versions are: vol-normalization, intraday recalculation, index-first framing, and an explicit public methodology. Volland and OptionsDepth are already marketing against sign-convention GEX; the counter is honesty, not silence — and honesty is already your house style.

2 · Where GEX works — and where your app currently points

Practitioner + academic consensus: the signal is most usable on SPX/SPY/QQQ (deep, 0DTE-dominated, flow-diverse) and least reliable on single names, where earnings and concentrated flow break the sign convention. ~56% of retail options volume is now ≤5 DTE. Convexa's deliberate 7–45 DTE swing framing is a legitimate niche (it sidesteps the 0DTE arms race Gexbot wins), but the app's any-ticker single-name emphasis points at the weakest part of the evidence. The scanner universe concept fits an index+liquid-megacap framing well.

3 · Why people pay, why they cancel

  • They pay for a daily ritual and live levels — SpotGamma's founder's note, Gexbot's chart-integrated real-time ladders — and for feeling institutional. Nobody in the sampled communities credibly attributes audited P&L improvement to any GEX tool.
  • They cancel on lag, price, and hindsight-bias: "realtime was wrong", "static pre-open levels", "$600/yr is spendy", "levels work until they don't". Billing/refund friction is the #2 complaint archetype (SpotGamma refunds, UW "all sales final", MenthorQ cutting access on auto-renew cancellation).

4 · The AI-advice trust problem

Adoption is high, trust is shallow: ~62% of surveyed retail investors have used AI to inform decisions, but only 23% trust it beyond "somewhat", and over half of those who acted on AI stock picks report a bad outcome. FINRA's experiment found only parity-level willingness to act on AI vs human information. Meanwhile the SEC's first AI-enforcement actions (Delphia, Global Predictions) targeted overstated AI-investing claims specifically, and FINRA's 2025 oversight report added a dedicated AI section.

Framing rule

"AI that explains today's dealer positioning in plain English" is the high-trust, low-regulatory-risk framing. "AI tells you what to trade" is the lowest-trust, highest-risk framing available. Convexa's canon already does the right thing internally (advisory-only, assumptions-carried, risk-first, no auto-act, mandatory confirm, educational disclaimers) — make the marketing copy match that discipline exactly, and mirror Options AI's disclaimer structure.

Assets

What you genuinely have (the pros)

  • An unoccupied intersection. Nobody — literally nobody in the researched set — combines dealer levels + live flow + an interpretation layer (setups/score) + structured AI reads + a paper-trading book + outcome tracking in one loop. UW is explicitly criticized for having no signals; SpotGamma has no paper trading; Gexbot has no interpretation. The synthesis is your product, not the GEX.
  • The track-record wedge is wide open. Every competitor's performance claim is a backtest or self-reported (Tickeron's 68–83%, Danelfin's backtests, Cheddar's vendor-adjacent 63%) and reviewers discount all of it. Convexa's outcome ledger — Wilson intervals, no window-shopping, closed-only, nothing silently excluded — is the only architecture in the field built to publish an honest forward record. That is simultaneously the differentiation and the compliance-safe posture.
  • Freshness is on the right side of the #1 complaint. Live SSE (NBBO mid, 5-min net flow, live analytic flip), 120s chain refresh on active sessions, 1.2s warm loads, honest live-vs-stale states. The market's loudest complaint is staleness; your architecture was built against it.
  • Honesty as engineering, matching the market's distrust. The promoted invariants read like direct answers to the review corpus: absent-is-not-none ↔ fabricated-claim complaints · model-read-carries-its-assumptions ↔ "imperfect heuristics" criticism · outcome ledger ↔ "hindsight bias". This is marketable, not just admirable — a public "how we compute this and where it fails" page would be unique in the niche.
  • Unit economics that scale correctly. BYO Anthropic keys mean AI cost scales with users, not with you; derived-analytics architecture (never redistributing raw quotes) is the one OPRA-viable indie shape; server-side gates and per-seat sessions already exist (the group-buy resale problem incumbents suffer is pre-answered).
  • A multi-leg model better than the journaling incumbents'. TraderSync mishandles ITM expirations; Edgewonk can't model spreads at all. Convexa's leg-first book, structure recognizer, and decision-linked ledger journal the decision, not just the fill — no incumbent does.
  • Sequencing discipline that de-risks the broker step. The model rebuild shipped before the importer, so SnapTrade import lands on a representation that can hold what it imports.
  • A development system that is itself an asset. The council/QA/ledger pipeline produced 35 shipped features with de-correlated verification as a solo operator. Whatever happens to Convexa commercially, this capability compounds.

Liabilities

Where the gaps are (the honest cons)

  1. Distribution is zero, and it is the whole game. No X presence, no Discord, no content engine, no waitlist; the live site is the June 29 build. Every survivor's path was identical: free daily artifact on X → Discord community → paid tier, with near-zero CAC. Gexbot posted free GEX charts for ~16 months before charging. There is no feature that substitutes for this.
  2. A data-licensing landmine sits under the first paying user. Massive's individual plans ($29–199/mo) are personal-use licenses; powering a customer-facing product requires Options Business at $1,999/mo. The indie-viable alternates: Databento OPRA at $199/mo (compute greeks yourself — which would also unify your two-gamma-source split and enable intraday vol-normalized GEX), ThetaData commercial (unpublished, they court fintechs), or Tradier's BYO-account entitlement. Server-side "calculations on behalf of customers" also brushes OPRA's non-display category — get vendor guidance in writing before charging anyone.
  3. The headline metric carries the field's weakest methodology. Vendor greeks + OI sign convention = the same naive GEX the evidence discredits and rivals attack. Mitigations are known (index-first, vol-normalize, intraday recalc, methodology page) but unshipped.
  4. Table-stakes features are missing: alerts (every rival has them; your levels/score/tier are alert-shaped already) and charting export (TradingView/ThinkorSwim level export is the single most universal integration — traders live in their charts, not your app). Mobile/PWA is behind that.
  5. Single-browser durable stores cap the product. The entire trading history lives in one browser's localStorage — no sync, no multi-device, tied to nothing. You've flagged it yourself (#21); it becomes acute the day a second user matters.
  6. No commercial spine: no billing/subscriptions, no per-seat plans, no onboarding, no ToS/disclaimer page, no marketing site beyond the splash. All solvable; all unstarted.
  7. The score is still unvalidated — by design you built the ledger to measure it, but today the 0–100 and the tier bands are hand-tuned constants. Until N accumulates, don't market the score; market the levels, the read, and the honesty.
  8. Solo-operator concentration risk. One person, one data vendor, one LLM vendor. The provider port and LLM seam mitigate architecturally, but commercially this is a bus-factor-one subscription product — worth being honest about in your own planning.

Priorities

What to focus on immediately

  1. Decide what this is. Personal instrument, indie business, or portfolio piece. Everything below assumes "indie business"; if it's the others, you're already done — keep building for your own edge and skip the rest.
  2. Deploy. The live site is five weeks behind main, missing two data-loss fixes and all security hardening. Run the manual runbook, set ALLOWED_ORIGINS, then automate. Nothing else on this list is possible against a stale build. (This was already your own #1 open item; the research only raises the stakes.)
  3. Resolve data licensing before the first dollar. Written vendor guidance on serving derived analytics commercially. Evaluate Databento OPRA $199/mo + in-house greeks seriously — it fixes cost, license, the two-gamma-source split, and enables the intraday vol-normalized GEX the evidence actually supports. This is a provider-port swap; your architecture anticipated it.
  4. Reposition: index-first, freshness-forward, methodology-open. Lead with SPX/SPY/QQQ + liquid megacaps (the scanner universe is this already). Publish a "how we compute this, and where it fails" page — no rival dares. Reframe every AI touchpoint as explains, never tells you what to trade; keep the educational disclaimer posture everywhere public.
  5. Start the distribution engine now, before more features. The proven playbook, in order: an X account posting one free daily artifact (an honest morning levels-read for SPY/QQQ — your event calendar + levels + regime read generates it automatically); a Discord; a free tier of the app (the scanner table is a natural free surface). Target: a habit, not virality. Gexbot took ~16 months of free posts; UW's free tier feeds 405k newsletter readers.
  6. Make the outcome ledger your public trust weapon. Run the AI rec + score on the universe daily, record every call in the ledger, and publish the rolling track record — wins, losses, Wilson intervals, no window-shopping. "We publish our own track record and nobody else will" is the sharpest sentence available to you in this market, and your architecture is the only one built to say it safely.
  7. Ship the two table-stakes features: alerts (level-touch, tier-change, score-gate — the plumbing exists) and TradingView level export (a webhook/copyable-levels endpoint is enough to start). These close the two loudest "why not" objections a trialing user will have.
  8. Then the commercial spine: Stripe + plans + ToS/disclaimers, account-scoped position storage (your #21 — becomes mandatory with real users), and SnapTrade read-only import (the model is ready for it; it's the retention feature, because imported real positions are the switching cost).

Sequencing note: items 2–4 are days each, not weeks. Item 5 is a standing daily habit, not a project. Items 6–8 are your next three delivery-pipeline features, in that order — and they displace the queued small fixes and `broker-connect` until the funnel exists.

Operating guidance

How to get the best results

A realistic 90-day shape

  • Weeks 1–2: deploy + licensing decision + AI-copy reframe + methodology page. Start daily X posts (automated artifact, hand-touched).
  • Weeks 3–6: alerts + level export ship; free tier defined (scanner + delayed levels); waitlist/beta invite loop into Discord.
  • Weeks 7–12: public track record page goes live with ≥20 recorded outcomes; billing + $29–39 founding-member tier; first 10 paying users recruited by hand from the Discord.

Pricing posture

  • Free tier that's genuinely useful (the funnel), paid at $29–49/mo — under the UW anchor, above the commodity belt, honest about being one ticker deep and index-first.
  • Generous trial + no-questions refund — the anti-pattern to the niche's #2 complaint, and it costs you almost nothing at this scale.
  • Later: an MCP/API surface for AI-agent traders — nascent channel, only UW and TradingVolatility are there, and you're natively positioned for it.

Metrics that tell you the truth

  • Leading: daily-artifact impressions → Discord joins → free-tier signups (the funnel exists or it doesn't).
  • Product: D30 return rate of free users (the "ritual" test — this niche retains on habit), levels-page opens per user per week.
  • Business: free→paid conversion, 3-month churn (expect brutal; structural washout is 70–90%/yr), MRR vs the ~$550/mo floor.
  • Honesty: the track record itself. If the score's hit rate is indistinguishable from noise at N≥100, say so publicly and sell the data + honesty, not the score.

Kill / continue criteria (pre-commit now)

  • Continue if by day 90: a daily posting habit held, ≥300 Discord members or ≥1k followers, ≥50 active free users, ≥10 paying.
  • Reassess if the funnel exists but conversion doesn't — usually a positioning problem, cheap to iterate.
  • Kill the business (keep the instrument) if after 6 months of consistent distribution effort the funnel won't fill — the product keeps its personal value either way, which is this project's real downside protection.

The one-paragraph summary: Convexa is a genuinely differentiated synthesis in a crowded but shallow market — better engineered than most incumbents, aimed at the two complaints (staleness, hindsight-bias) users actually churn on, with the only honest-track-record architecture in the field. It is worth developing if the next quarter is spent on deployment, licensing, positioning and distribution rather than features. The market's economics cap the realistic outcome at a healthy indie business; the downside is protected because the instrument keeps its personal trading value and the delivery system keeps its career value regardless. The riskiest path is the current one: building excellent software nobody can see.
