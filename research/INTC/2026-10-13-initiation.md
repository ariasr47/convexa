Intel designs x86 processors and makes most of them in its own fabs, which it is now trying to fill with other companies' chips too. At $101.35 the shares price both halves at full health: product margins near their best and a foundry that earns money from outside customers it has not yet named. Whether Intel Foundry signs those customers for 14A, and stops losing about $2B a quarter, decides what the equity is worth.

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs record revenue at peak margins, from a foundry that has yet to earn its keep.

At $101.35 the market values the operations at about $582B (calc), roughly nine times FY2026 revenue and 5.5× the $105.7B of net property, plant and equipment on the June 27 balance sheet [2]. Intel's best years, 2018–2020, ran 30–33% operating margins on $71–78B of revenue [9]. The price needs more than that.

# Valuation and what's next

## Valuation | On evidence-based inputs every scenario sits well below the price; the margin target decides most of it.

The method is a turnaround DCF on the consolidated business: an explicit operating-margin path from 2027 to a mature level by 2031, reinvestment through sales to capital, then subtract partners' claims. Free cash flow turns positive once margin gains outrun the capex ramp, from 2028 in the base case. The failure probability is zero because the ratings are investment grade and cash about matches debt.

- **Uncertainty is very high, not extreme.** Intel is investment grade and cash about matches debt, so the equity is not a thin slice over debt. The spread comes from one yes-or-no question, whether the foundry signs outside customers, and the margins that follow from the answer.
- **The price sits above the whole range, and so does Wall Street's average target.** Even the bull case, 2018–2020 margins on about twice the 2021 peak revenue by 2031 (calc), falls short. At base margins, the growth the price implies is above the 45%-a-year pace that only 1.3% of large companies held for five years [38].
