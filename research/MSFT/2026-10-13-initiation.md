Microsoft sells Office, Windows and the Azure cloud, and it now spends 44 cents of each revenue dollar on data centers and AI chips. At $541 the price assumes that build-out keeps revenue compounding faster than in any stretch of the past decade, at margins near today's. What decides it is how much revenue each dollar of new capital brings back: $1.91 over FY2019–22, $0.46 in FY2026 (calc). Fiscal years end June 30, so FQ1-27 is July–September 2026.

## Case for and against | A franchise with pricing power, against a capital bill that keeps rising.


## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has fallen by a third. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
Enterprise AI budgets paused in 2027. Azure growth fell from the mid-40s to the mid-20s just as capacity signed in 2025–26 kept arriving. | #1

FY2027 operating margin came in below 45% as opex grew with the build-out, and FY2028 guidance cut it again. | #3
OpenAI moved a large share of new inference to other clouds and renegotiated its Azure commitment down. | #4
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing a decade in which AI capacity earns like FY2024's (0.83 of revenue per dollar) and Copilot seat growth keeps compounding. That is roughly this note's bull case, which still lands below the price at today's cost of capital.
