NVIDIA sells most of the chips, networking and software that AI data centers run on, and it keeps about two-thirds of its revenue as operating profit. Its own guide puts FY2028 revenue about 70% higher, so the price rests on what follows: growth that companies this size almost never keep up, and margins that stay far above NVIDIA's pre-AI level while its largest buyers design their own chips.

- **Where it came from.** Hyperscale revenue was $48.7B (+102% YoY) and AI clouds, industrial and enterprise (ACIE) $40.3B (+138%) [2]. Blackwell is still most of revenue; Vera Rubin began shipping in August and should be about 20% of data-center revenue in Q3 [2][3].
- **China is priced out.** H200 sales under US licenses were under 1% of data-center revenue. They pass a US inspection that brings a 25% import tariff, which NVIDIA absorbs, and H1 carried a $0.4B H200 charge [4]. The outlook assumes no China data-center compute [1].
- **Revenue per gigawatt.** Management puts its opportunity at about $18B per gigawatt for Hopper, $25B for Blackwell and $40B for Vera Rubin, which adds CPUs, networking and Groq LPUs [3].

# The debate

## Case for and against | Supply-limited demand against buyers who are building their own chips.

```case
+ Demand exceeds supply into FY2028 | Growth of about 70% is guided and supply-limited; customers' forecasts point to growth doubling. Top-5 hyperscaler capex is expected near $1.3T in 2027, from about $800B. [3]
+ More of each data center | Revenue per gigawatt rises from $25B with Blackwell to $40B with Vera Rubin. CPU revenue should more than double in FY2028. [3]
+ A broader customer base | The largest direct customer fell from 23% to 16% of revenue in a year. ACIE grew 138% and should be about half of data-center revenue. [3][4]
+ The cash is real | First-half free cash flow was $69.9B on a 66% operating margin. Half or more goes back to shareholders. [1][3]
- The biggest buyers are building their own chips | Broadcom has line of sight to ~$230B of AI revenue in FY2028. Anthropic plans 5 GW of TPUs in 2027, and OpenAI 1.3 GW of its own chip. [16]
- NVIDIA increasingly finances its own demand | $99B of stakes, $25B more committed, up to $108.5B of guarantees and $36B of capacity backstops. The labs it backs are about a quarter of next year's business. [2][3][4]
- Margins have peaked | Gross margin steps down from 75% to 71–72% in Q4 and 72–73% in FY2028 as memory costs rise. [3]
- Concentrated on one capex cycle | Three direct customers bought 44% of first-half revenue (calc). AI spending has never been through a downturn at this scale. [4]
```

## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has halved. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
Power, land and memory limited builds, and NVIDIA cut its FY2028 outlook to about 40% growth. Estimates for FY2029 fell with it. | #1
Memory costs kept rising and Rubin met TPU and MI450 price competition. Gross margin fell below 70% and the market stopped paying for 60%+ margins. | #2
An AI lab NVIDIA backs failed to raise its next round. Payment terms stretched, a capacity backstop was triggered, and investors discounted the financed quarter of demand. | #3
Anthropic and OpenAI moved most new capacity to their own chips, and hyperscale revenue fell for a quarter. | #4
A hyperscaler cut 2027 capex after AI revenue lagged its spending, and the others followed within two quarters. | #5
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing a second year of ~70% growth if Rubin ramps as guided and the labs' IPOs fund the next wave, while this note waits for a slowdown that comes later than assumed.
