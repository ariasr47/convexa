TSMC takes about 72% of the world's foundry revenue, and AI chips have lifted its operating margin to 60%, from 50% a year earlier. At $444 per ADR, about 20 times next year's expected earnings, the price works only if growth stays strong for years after 2027. What decides it is whether $60B-plus a year of new fabs keeps earning today's returns; Taiwan is the risk no input in the model captures.

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs years of fast growth after 2027; the base case slows sooner.

```implied
```

The base case takes 2027 revenue at the consensus average, then grows it 9% a year to 2031, the pace that lands on management's own plan of about 25% a year from 2024 to 2029 [10][18]. To reach today's price at base margins and cost of capital, growth in 2028–31 would have to run far faster than 9%, and for four more years after a 2027 already expected to grow 36% [18].

Capitalizing today's profit covers only a minority of the price. The rest is growth the market expects but no one has yet booked. In the Base Rate Book's 1950–2015 sample, only 9% of companies grew sales faster than 15% a year over ten years, after inflation, and the largest companies grew slowest [35].

# The debate

## Case for and against | AI demand and a widening lead against peak capex, margin dilution and Taiwan.

```case
+ AI runs through TSMC | HPC rose to 66% of revenue in 2Q26 from 60% a year earlier. Management plans AI accelerator revenue growth in the mid-to-high 50s % a year to 2029 and now calls it stronger. [1][2][10]
```

## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the ADR has halved. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
Hyperscalers paused AI spending in 2027 after a year of digestion. TSMC's January 2027 guide called for growth well below 20%, and the multiple collapsed with the growth. | #1
2nm, A16 and the Arizona ramp pushed gross margin below 60% while wafer price rises stalled. Earnings stopped growing even as revenue did. | #2
A top customer moved a flagship AI chip to Samsung's Texas 2nm line for tariff relief, and others began dual-sourcing. | #3
Capex reached $80B-plus in 2027 but revenue lagged, and revenue per dollar of fabs fell toward 1.0×. | #4
A Chinese "quarantine" of Taiwan lasted more than a week. No fab was hit, but the market began pricing the tail. | #5
Monthly revenue fell year over year in two straight months as AI inventory built up at customers. | #6
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing a compounder if AI demand keeps 2028–31 growth near management's 25% plan and margins hold near 60%. The price already leans that way, which is why growth is the sensitivity grid's row axis.

How to read it:

- **Growth after 2027 is the trigger.** It moves the base value most, so it forms the grid's rows. The through-cycle margin is next. The cost of capital and sales-to-capital move it less across their sourced ranges.
- **All three cases sit below the price.** Even the bull case, with consensus-high 2027 and 15% growth to 2031, falls short. The gap is mostly growth beyond 2027: at base margins and cost of capital, the price needs growth far above 9% for four years.
- **What the model leaves out.** Year 0 is FY2026, so 4Q26's cash flow is ignored: one quarter's free cash flow, NT$287B in 2Q26 [1]. The value is per ADR-equivalent of the company's cash flows; Taipei shares sell about 10% below the ADR (calc) [20][21]. With nothing left in a failure, each 10% of failure probability would cut every value by 10%.
