Alphabet owns Google Search, YouTube and Google Cloud, and in 2026 it will spend about $200B on data centers and chips, more than twice 2025's $91.4B [4][8]. At $352.72 the price needs revenue to keep growing at or above this year's pace for years, with that new capital earning the kind of returns search did. What decides it is whether Cloud's $514B backlog turns into profit faster than the depreciation bill arrives [2].

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs faster growth than Alphabet's best decade, on far more capital.

The base case below takes revenue for the year to June 2027 from consensus, then grows 12% a year, Alphabet's 2021–25 pace (calc) [27], before fading to 3% by year 10. To reach today's price, the base case would need growth in years 2–5 well above Alphabet's 2015–25 average of 18.3% a year (calc) [7][4], while capex runs at twice its 2025 level.

Today's operating profit, capitalized with no growth, covers only a minority of the price. The rest is growth the market expects, and in this model growth is paid for in capital: in 2026 each new dollar of revenue is costing about $1.75 of capex net of depreciation (calc) [4][8][26].

## Moat and competition | Courts found two monopolies; whether they survive AI answers is the open question.

```moat
Intangibles | evidence | A court found Google unlawfully maintained monopolies in general search and search text ads [12], and another court found the same in ad tech [14]. The Gemini app has 950M monthly users [8].
Cost advantage | evidence | Its own TPU chips, which outsiders now buy: Anthropic committed to up to 1M worth tens of billions of dollars [21], and TPU systems ship into customers' data centers [8]. Pre-tax return on capital ran 25–37% in every year checked from 2015 to 2025 (calc) [7][28].
Switching costs | unproven | Cloud's $514B backlog is contracted [2], but the search judgment bans exclusive default deals, the old lock-in [12].
Network effects | evidence | The search remedy forces Google to share its index and user data with rivals [12], which treats its scale of data as the barrier to entry.
Efficient scale | none | Search and cloud are contested by deep-pocketed rivals, and AI assistants compete for the same questions.
```

- **Capacity is the constraint.** Alphabet raised capex to bring capacity forward to meet demand [8], and also rents space in SpaceX's former xAI data centers near Memphis [22], while selling TPUs into other companies' data centers [8].
- **The returns record is changing shape.** Return on capital employed fell from 26.2% in FY2025 to 18.5% now, as the capital base grew faster than profit [28].

## Case for and against | A second growth engine against a capital bill with no precedent at Alphabet.


## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has halved. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem

TPU and GPU rental prices fell as everyone's new capacity came online, and Cloud's margin dropped back toward 20%. | #3
Two years of roughly $200B-plus capex landed as depreciation in 2027 while revenue growth slowed, and the operating margin fell below 30%. | #4

Capex kept outrunning cash flow, and Alphabet sold more stock beyond the tax-covering ATM. | #6
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing a decade in which Alphabet sells AI computing the way it sold search ads, with Cloud margins heading toward Services' 42% and the capex wave cresting in 2027.

How to read it:

- **The ranking of inputs.** Growth and the target margin move value most, so they form the grid. The terminal cost of capital comes next. The scenario cost of capital matters less, because it fades to the terminal rate after year 5.
- **Year 1 reinvestment is stated, not inferred.** It is guided capex less depreciation. After year 1 the model charges capital only as revenue grows, at each case's sales-to-capital ratio.
- **Timing.** The model counts from June 30, 2026 and books each year's cash at year-end, while the price is from October 12. A mid-year convention would lift every value by about 5% (calc), not enough to change the ranking against the price.
- **The distress value**, $51 a share, is book value per common share (calc) [2]. It is unused at 0% failure.
