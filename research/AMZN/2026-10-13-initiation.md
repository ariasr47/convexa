Amazon runs the largest online store and the largest cloud, AWS, which brings in 19% of revenue but 58% of operating profit (calc). At $2.8T the price assumes that the roughly $220B a year now going into AI data centers earns well above its cost. The deciding question is what AWS earns once that capacity starts to depreciate, and how much revenue each dollar of capex brings in.

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs the AI build-out to earn well above its cost, and to start soon.

```implied
```

The base case below follows consensus for the next year, then grows revenue 12% a year to year 5, close to Amazon's 11.7% average for 2023–25 (calc) [14][10][23]. At the base case's margins and capital turns, reaching the price takes much faster growth than that. Today's operating profit, capitalized, covers only a minority of the price; the rest is growth the market pays for in advance.

That growth isn't free. Each extra dollar of revenue needs new data centers, and in the twelve months to June Amazon added about $1.04 of revenue per dollar of net capex (calc), against $1.94 over 2023–25 [3][10]. The price is a bet that this ratio recovers while AWS keeps its margins.

## Moat and competition | AWS contracts lock customers in for years; there's no long record of returns above the cost of capital.

```moat
Intangibles | evidence | Prime and shopping data support a $76.1B-a-year ad business growing 26% (calc). [3]
Cost advantage | unproven | Same-day and overnight deliveries grew over 40% in Q2, but store margins were −2.4% as recently as 2022 (calc). Trainium's cost edge over GPUs has no independent measurement. [1][14]
Switching costs | evidence | $496B of AWS contracts longer than a year, averaging 6.4 years. AWS margins have stayed between 27% and 39% since 2022 (calc). [2][14][3]
Network effects | evidence | Third-party seller services brought in $46.8B in Q2, up 16%. Sellers come for the buyers, and buyers for the selection. [3]
Efficient scale | unproven | Spending $220B a year is a high bar to entry, but scale didn't stop AWS's margin falling to 27% in 2023 (calc). No market-share data was gathered for this note. [14][4]
```

Returns on capital have been uneven. In 2022 Amazon's return on invested capital was 6.2% and its return on capital employed 4.3%, below a cost of capital of about 9–10%. Both have been above it since 2023 [25]. Three good years aren't a long record, so the valuation lets new investment after year 10 earn only its cost of capital.

Regulation is the other open front. The FTC's 2023 monopolization suit is still pending, and the sources gathered for this note give no trial date [36].

# The debate

## Case for and against | Contracted AI demand on one side; capex outrunning cash on the other.

```case
+ AWS is speeding up on contracted demand | Growth went from 17% to 37% in a year. Backlog reached $496B with a 6.4-year life, and most AI capacity is signed for five years or more. [2][3][5]
+ Advertising raises store margins | Ads were $76.1B over 12 months, up 26% in Q2. Store margins went from −2.4% in 2022 to 6.2% (calc). [3][14]

- Capex is outrunning cash | Twelve-month capex of $173.0B exceeds operating cash flow of $161.4B. Debt nearly doubled in six months. [1][2]
- Two big customers are also investees | OpenAI's and Anthropic's commitments since February top $200B. Amazon funds both, partly against compute milestones. [2][15][17]
- Depreciation hasn't arrived yet | D&A was $75.2B over 12 months against $177.1B of capex. Servers depreciate over five to six years, and memory costs pushed the guide up 10%. [3][4][8]

```

## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has fallen 40%. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
The AI labs ran into funding limits. Contracts were stretched out, backlog stopped growing, and AWS growth fell back to about 20%. | #1

Backlog shrank for the first time after one lab cut its commitment. | #3
2026 capex overshot the guide on memory costs, and the 2027 guide rose again while sales grew in the mid-teens. Free cash flow stayed negative and debt kept rising. | #4

Anthropic listed below its private mark and Amazon's stake was written down. | #6
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing the run if the backlog converts on schedule and AWS holds its margins while capex intensity falls in 2028.

How to read it:

- **The ranking of inputs.** The long-run margin moves value far more than anything else; growth and sales to capital come next, about equally. The grid crosses margin with sales to capital because together they answer the question here: does the capex earn its cost?
- **Why the bull case still falls short.** Most of the value comes after year 10, and every scenario charges a year of heavy capex up front. A price above the bull case needs a margin above 20%, a lower cost of capital, or capital turns better than 2023–25's.
- **The stakes are a small part.** At June 30 carrying values, after tax, they're about $17 a share (calc). An Anthropic IPO near the reported ~$2T target would add materially more, and none of that is counted here [19].
