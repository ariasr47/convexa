Meta sells ads to the 3.6 billion people who use its apps each day, and revenue grew 28% last quarter. It is also spending $130–145B this year on AI infrastructure, about 54% of expected revenue. The price assumes that spending earns a high return; the one thing that decides it is how much revenue each new dollar of capital brings back once depreciation lands in the income statement.

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs growth, margins and capital efficiency close to Meta's 2020–25 record.


The base case below keeps consensus for the year to June 2027, then grows revenue 13% a year while margins settle at 30% as depreciation catches up. To reach today's price it needs far faster growth than that, faster than the 18.5% a year Meta managed from 2020 to 2025 (calc) [9][6].

Most of the price is a bet on future growth, not on today's profit. That growth only adds value if new capital earns more than it costs. In 2016–23 each dollar of net new property brought $1.22 of revenue; in 2026 it brings about $0.48 (calc) [9][8][1].

## Moat and competition | Network effects are real; whether AI adds a new advantage is unproven.

```moat
Intangibles | unproven | Muse Wonder, launched Oct 12, is what Meta calls its most capable model, with no pricing or benchmark detail [14]. No sign yet that it beats rivals' models.
Cost advantage | unproven | Over 1GW of its own chips with Broadcom, plus AMD, aim at cheaper compute [5]. Rivals build at the same scale, and 2026's margins don't show an edge yet [1].
Switching costs | unproven | Advertisers can move budgets weekly. Advantage+ automation, at a $75B+ run rate, deepens reliance on Meta's tools [2].
Network effects | evidence | 3.6B daily people, Instagram at 2B daily actives [2]. Ad prices rose 12% while impressions grew 14%, so more users and data still raise the value of each ad [1].
Efficient scale | none | Digital advertising is contested by Google, Amazon, TikTok and others; no market is too small for a rival.
```

The record supports a durable edge in the ad business. Return on capital ran 33–49% in 2016–20 (calc from [9]) and 27–46% in 2021–25 [21], far above any plausible cost of capital. That record predates the AI build, which is why the valuation keeps some excess return after year 10 but far less than the history.

How to read it:

- **Year 1 carries the build.** About $146B of net capex in one year is about $57 a share (calc). The ratio would charge far less for a year when capex runs ahead of revenue, so it's stated outright, as the model allows.
- **Margin is the lever.** The year-5 margin and the growth rate move value most, so they form the sensitivity grid. Sales to capital and the cost of capital matter less. Each scenario pairs its margin with a capex path: heavy spending means more depreciation and a lower margin.
- **Timing.** Year 0 ends Jun 30, 2026, about 3.5 months before the price date, which understates value by roughly 3% (calc). Counting the ventures as debt costs about $13 a share (calc).
