Micron makes memory chips, and an AI-driven shortage has lifted its gross margin to a record 87%, from −9% three years ago. At 5.6 times next year's expected earnings the stock looks cheap only if those margins last, so this note lets them fall back to a through-cycle level instead of capitalizing the peak.

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs the boom to keep growing for years; the last two cycles turned within a year.

```implied
```

The base case below keeps FY2027 at consensus, then lets prices and margins normalize by FY2031. To reach today's price it needs revenue to keep growing after FY2027 instead of falling back. In the last two cycles revenue fell within a year of record margins: −23% in FY2019 and −49% in FY2023 (calc) [7][8].

Capitalizing FY2026's profit forever covers most of the price, but FY2026 was the peak. Reading a low multiple of peak earnings as cheap is the classic cyclical mistake [21].

```variant
How long prices stay high | Revenue keeps growing after FY2027 rather than falling back. | | TrendForce's quarterly contract-price calls [15]; the FQ2-27 margin guide in December.
Through-cycle margin | Margins settle far above the 17% ten-year average. | | Whether contract floors hold when spot prices next fall below them.
Customer contracts | Floors and deposits make the next downturn shallow. | | Any repricing, cancellation or early deposit refund in a 10-Q or 8-K.
New supply | More than $50B a year of capex earns peak-like returns instead of flooding the market. | | Days of inventory, rival fab start dates [24], CXMT's server DRAM [13].
```

Gross margin has peaked three times in ten years (FY2018, FY2022, FY2026) and collapsed twice. FY2023's trough came one year after a 45% year [7][8][9][3]. Annual DRAM and NAND prices moved anywhere from about +200% to about −50% over the past five years [3]. The FQ1-27 guide is the first step down, and it was below the ~87% analysts expected [23].

The ten years from FY2016 to FY2025 hold two troughs (FY2016, FY2023) and two peaks (FY2018, FY2022), so they make the through-cycle anchor. The valuation's margin targets are built from these numbers.

## Contracts and customer deposits | About $150B of contracted revenue, and $12.7B of deposits that must be paid back.


- **The contracts.** 26 strategic customer agreements (SCAs) are take-or-pay and cover an estimated 35%+ of revenue through 2030. Three-quarters of that has a defined price, mostly a floor-and-ceiling band [2]. RPO is about $150B at minimum prices [2].
- **The ceiling.** The largest SCAs cap existing products at the calendar-Q2 2026 market price, and terms typically run through CY2030 [5].
- **The deposits.** $12.3B arrived in FQ4-26 as financing cash flow, not free cash flow [2]. They're repaid mostly in 2029–31 if customers meet their minimum purchases [3], so they're debt-like here. Total customer commitments are $32B, mostly cash deposits [2].

# The debate

## Case for and against | Contracts and AI demand against a record of every boom ending in a glut.

```case
+ Contracts change the shape of the cycle | 26 take-or-pay SCAs, about $150B of RPO at minimum prices and $32B of customer commitments. Floor prices give gross margins well above any past peak quarter, management says. [2][5]
+ Supply looks tight through 2028 | Management expects CY27–28 to be much tighter than 2026, with no line of sight to balance. More than 75% of FY2027 output is committed. [2][14]
+ AI has changed the mix | Data center is about 60% of revenue. Data center SSDs were nearly $10B in FQ4-26, more than 10× a year earlier. [2][3]
+ The cash is real | FY2026 adjusted free cash flow was $62.3B, excluding deposits. Capital return steps up from Dec 9. [1][2]
- Every memory boom has ended in a glut | Annual prices swung from about +200% to about −50% in five years. FY2023's gross margin was −9.1%, a year after 45.2%. [3][8]
- Ceilings cap the upside; floors are untested | The largest SCAs cap existing products at the calendar-Q2 2026 price. No floor has yet met a falling market. [5]
- Capacity is being added at the peak | FY2027 capex is guided above $50B, and SK hynix reportedly aims to nearly double DRAM wafer capacity by 2030. [2][24]
- HBM share is slipping | Micron's HBM revenue share fell from 21% to 18% in Q2 CY26 while Samsung's rose from 21% to 33%. [17]
```

## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has halved. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
New cleanrooms at SK hynix, Samsung and Micron arrived in 2028 just as AI buyers digested their spending. Contract prices turned in early 2028 and the market priced the trough a year ahead. | #1
FQ2-27 gross margin came in below the FQ1 "floor" as price increases stalled, and estimates for FY2028 fell. | #2
In the first price decline, the largest customers pushed to reprice, and Micron gave ground to keep volumes above the take-or-pay minimums. | #3
Samsung's HBM4 won the next NVIDIA platform and Micron's HBM share fell into the low teens. | #4
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing the run if supply stays tight into 2029 and SCAs reprice higher, while this note waits for a bust that doesn't come.

How to read it:

- **The ranking of inputs.** The growth path and the through-cycle margin move value most, so they form the sensitivity grid. Cost of capital matters less, because most of the value arrives in the first five years.
- **Year 1 reinvestment is stated, not inferred.** FY2027's revenue jump comes from price, not capacity, so the sales-to-capital ratio would overcharge it. The note uses FY2027's guided capex less depreciation, plus working capital, instead. After year 1 the model charges capital only when revenue rises, which here happens only near year 10. In the last downturn capex fell to about depreciation (FY2024: $8.4B of capex against $7.8B) [3], so a zero net charge is close to history. It does leave out construction already committed for fabs opening in 2028–30 [2], which flatters years 2–5.
- **Deposits are debt.** They came in as financing, so free cash flow never counted them [2]. They leave net cash here because they're owed back. Book value of $122 a share (calc) [3] is the distress value, which is unused at 0% failure.
