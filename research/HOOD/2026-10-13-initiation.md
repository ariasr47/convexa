Robinhood is a retail broker that earns from trading (options, stocks, crypto and prediction markets), from interest on customer cash and margin loans, and from subscriptions. At 48 times trailing earnings the price assumes growth well beyond the 25% a year it compounded from 2021 to 2025, for years. What decides it is whether today's record retail activity is a new floor or the top of a cycle that cut revenue by a quarter in 2022.

# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price needs growth far above Robinhood's own record pace, for years.

The base case below takes next year's revenue from Wall Street's 2026 estimate, grows it 10% a year after that, and lets the operating margin settle at a through-cycle 39%. To reach today's price it needs revenue growth far above the 25% a year Robinhood compounded from 2021 to 2025 (calc), held for four more years [4][7]. Even the bull case, which keeps that 25% pace and today's margin, stays well below the price.

Capitalizing today's profit with no growth covers only a small part of the price. Analysts expect Q3 revenue up about 11% on a year earlier [17], so the near-term pace is slowing, not speeding up.

- **What counts.** Corporate cash and equivalents of $5,362M [2], less the $2.2B of 0% convertible notes due Oct 1, 2029 at face [11][12]. The notes convert at about $174.42 a share, well above the price, so they are debt here. The retained stake in Robinhood Ventures Fund I is added at its $437M fair value on June 30 [2].
- **What doesn't.** $12.0B of cash segregated for customers, $22.8B of receivables from customers (mostly margin loans), $6.0B of securities borrowed and $4.8B of customers' fractional shares all belong to the brokerage balance sheet. So do the matching $17.2B owed to users and $20.5B of securities loaned [2].
- **A caveat.** Part of the corporate cash sits in regulated broker-dealers, which must hold minimum net capital and need FINRA approval to pay out more than 10% of excess capital [5]. Treating all of it as spare flatters net cash a little.


## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has halved. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
Markets fell in early 2027, margin calls shrank the margin book, and options and stock volumes dropped back toward 2024 levels. Trading revenue fell below $600M in a quarter. | #1
A federal court or the CFTC barred sports event contracts for retail customers, and event revenue halved within two quarters. | #2
Savers left for higher bank rates as deposit matches stopped working, and net deposits slowed to single digits annualized. | #3
```

The other direction: the cost of passing would be missing a structural shift. If younger investors, tokenized assets and prediction markets keep retail participation higher for good, the 25% history continues and the base case here looks too cautious.

How to read it:

- **The ranking of inputs.** Growth and the through-cycle margin move value most, so they form the sensitivity grid. Cost of capital and sales to capital matter less: between them, the full bear-to-bull range moves the base value far less than growth does.
- **Year 1 starts mid-2026.** Year 0 is the four quarters to June 2026, so year 1 ends in June 2027, about eight months after the price date. The model discounts it a full year, which is slightly conservative.
- **What is left out.** The Rothera stake has no disclosed value and isn't added. The capped call is ignored and the notes are counted at face. Neither changes the picture.
