At $594 the company is worth about $1 trillion, around 20 times this year's expected sales, which only works if AMD takes a large slice of AI accelerators from Nvidia and custom chips. The MI450 and Helios ramp, shipping from now into 2027 for OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic, decides it.

The base case below takes 2027 at the analysts' average and then grows revenue 20% a year through 2031. At base-case margins, today's price needs far faster growth from 2028 (shown above), faster even than the bull case's 30%. Today's profit, capitalized, covers only a small slice of the price. Almost all of it is a bet on what AMD sells in 2027–2031, and most of that is AI accelerators that have not yet shipped in volume [3][21].

- **Data center did it.** Segment revenue went from $3.24B in Q2 2025 to $6.72B, while Client and Gaming rose 6% [1]. Instinct GPU sales more than doubled on the MI350 series; AMD doesn't report them separately [3].
- **One-offs.** Q2 2025 took an $800M MI308 export-control charge. Q4 2025 had a ~$360M reserve release and ~$390M of MI308 sales to China; without them its non-GAAP gross margin was ~55%, not 57% [5].

## GAAP vs adjusted | Amortization from past acquisitions is most of the gap; stock pay is the rest, and stays a cost here.

- **Below the line.** GAAP net income of $2.30B included $483M of investment gains [1]. The Q2 tax rate was 9.8%, helped by export-income deductions and R&D credits [2].

- **AI accelerators.** Even counting all of AMD's data center revenue as GPUs, it was 6.0% of the three companies' total (calc) [1][17][18]. NVIDIA's data center sold 13× as much (calc), and Broadcom guides its AI revenue to $21.7B next quarter [18].

## Moat and competition | Strong in x86 servers; in AI it is the second source, not the standard.

```moat
Intangibles | evidence | Its server designs took a record 46.2% of x86 server CPU revenue in Q1 2026 [20], and Venice is the first x86 server CPU on 2nm [21]. In GPUs the edge belongs to NVIDIA's CUDA; ROCm is narrowing the gap [21].
Cost advantage | unproven | Fabless, so no manufacturing scale of its own. Non-GAAP gross margin 56% against NVIDIA's 75% [1][17].
Switching costs | unproven | Buyers add AMD partly to avoid relying on one supplier, which limits loyalty to AMD too. Warrants helped secure the two largest commitments [6][8].
Network effects | none | Software ecosystems have them, but they favor CUDA; ROCm is the follower [21].
Efficient scale | unproven | x86 has two suppliers, but Arm already has 15.3% of PC processors [30] and NVIDIA sells its own Vera server CPU [21].
```

## Pre-mortem | It's October 2027 and the stock has halved. The likeliest stories why.

```premortem
Helios volume slipped to mid-2027 as HBM4 and rack integration ran late. 2027 data center growth came in near 60%, not 100%, and 2028 estimates fell. | #1
NVIDIA's Rubin racks and Broadcom's custom chips kept AMD near a 6% share of AI accelerators. The 2027 outlook disappointed and the multiple compressed. | #2

Rack-scale systems pulled gross margin into the low 50s while memory costs hit PC demand. | #4
```

The other direction: passing costs the most if MI450 ships on time, Anthropic and Microsoft scale up, and AMD becomes the standing second source in a market AMD itself sizes at $1.4T by 2030 [3]. Then today's price would prove a base, not a peak.

- **None has vested yet.** At Jun 27, 2026 no tranche had vested; the warrants are classed as liabilities and have not touched the income statement [2]. Tranche sizes and the intermediate price hurdles aren't disclosed [6][8].
- **Why count all 320M.** The base and bull cases assume these customers buy most of their 12 GW, which is what vests the warrants. The top price hurdle, $600, is about today's price. Exercise brings in only ~$3M (calc), so the shares are effectively free and belong in the count [29].
- **What it costs the bear.** With only the binding 2 GW vested, and tranches assumed even (~26.7M per GW), the bear count would be about 1,712M (calc). The single share count therefore understates the bear value.
- **Buybacks are small.** $9.2B of authorization is left, and AMD bought back just $221M in H1 2026 [2].
