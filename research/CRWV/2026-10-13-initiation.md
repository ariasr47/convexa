# The verdict

## What the price implies | The price pays almost twice the capital in the business, so it needs returns not yet shown.

At $80.26 the market values the operations at about $75.9B, ≈1.9× the $39.6B of net operating capital on the June 30 balance sheet (calc: PP&E $46.7B + receivables $2.5B − customer prepayments $9.7B) [1]. Paying twice the capital means expecting years of returns well above an ~11% cost of capital.

Faster growth does not close the gap. At the base case's economics, new capital earns about 6% after tax (calc: 15% margin × 0.55 sales to capital × 75%), so each extra dollar of growth lowers value. The price is a bet on margins after depreciation and on capital turns.

```variant
Margins after depreciation | Mature EBIT margins far above today's −2% GAAP, at or above management's mid-20s contract margin [7] | | Q4 2026 adjusted operating margin against "low teens"; 2027 GAAP operating margin

Cost of capital | Funding gets cheaper, heading toward investment grade | | S&P rating actions; the coupon on the next unsecured issue

```

# Valuation and what's next

## Valuation | On evidence-based inputs every scenario sits below the price; margins and capital turns decide it.

The method is an enterprise DCF: value the operations, subtract net debt, and weight in the chance of distress, anchored to credit ratings and pricing [15]. Net debt counts term loans, notes, convertibles and delayed-draw (DDTL) facilities. It leaves out operating leases, because their cost is already in the margins.

- **The two inputs that matter most are margin after depreciation and sales to capital.** The grid starts at the base case and steps toward better economics; below the base on either axis, every cell is zero. WACC moves the result much less, because a higher rate shrinks both the cash-burning build years and the terminal value.
- **Uncertainty is extreme, not very high.** The equity is a thin slice over the debt: a small error in what the business is worth moves the equity by a multiple of it, and in the weaker cases the going-concern equity is worth nothing.
- **The model sits far from Wall Street's average target.** That target values the EBITDA; this model charges for the GPUs that produce it and for the chance of distress.
