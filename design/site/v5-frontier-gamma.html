F1 · The dealer-gamma surface, in WebGL

The landing’s one hero instrument, Convexa’s own model, drawn in depth and turned by hand: strike across, expiry deep, dealer gamma up and down. The static projection is not a loading screen; it is the designed fallback, complete with labels and a table.

Budget, and what was measured

A mid laptop at 60 Hz: 16.7 ms per frame, about 10 ms of it ours. Measured in headless Chromium on this machine (WebGL through SwiftShader, a software rasteriser: a pessimistic stand-in for a GPU).

Fallbacks

WhenWhat the reader gets
WebGL2 availableThe live surface, drag to turn, the probe by keyboard or hover, labels in HTML at 11px.
No WebGL, a blocked script, or a lost contextThe poster: the same surface projected statically, the same labels, the readout and the table. The context comes back on restore.
Reduced motionNothing differs: nothing moves unless the reader moves it. No auto-rotate, no intro, no inertia.
PhoneA sideways drag turns it; a vertical swipe scrolls the page (touch-action pan-y). Pixel ratio capped at 1.5.
Hidden tab, off-screen, idleNo frames at all: a frame is drawn only after input, a resize or a scheme change.
Screen readerOne labelled group; arrows move the probe and the readout is announced; the table carries every level by expiry.

Canon it needs

P-15 · A runtime landing hero (owner ruling needed)

The canon rules the landing hero a static isometric SVG and says a runtime scene needs a fresh owner ruling (Amendments 2026-09-10, D-B (A)). F1 asks for it: WebGL2 with the static projection as its designed fallback, no dependency, render on demand, no autoplay motion (so the hero curve stays the one orchestrated moment). The flip line is phosphor, not amber: amber stays spent.
